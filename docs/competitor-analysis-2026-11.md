# Sthuthi Securities competitor analysis

Research date: 4 November 2026  
Scope: US-facing penetration testing, AI/LLM security assessment, PTaaS, SOC 2 readiness, and security-questionnaire services

## Executive decision

Sthuthi should not copy the large PTaaS companies' breadth. Cobalt, BreachLock, NetSPI, and HackerOne sell platform access, fast scheduling, large testing capacity, integrations, and repeatable portfolio coverage. Bishop Fox and Trail of Bits sell specialist depth, research reputation, and experienced teams. Small compliance shops compete with fixed fees and direct founder access.

Sthuthi can occupy a narrower position:

> Fixed-scope security testing for software teams that need usable evidence for an enterprise customer, an audit, or an AI launch. Published starting prices, written boundaries, engineer-ready findings, and one retest.

The current product already supports that position better than a scale claim. It shows authorization, scope, evidence provenance, review state, remediation state, and retest state. The missing part is market proof: named practitioners, credentials, insurance, case studies, report acceptance, and real delivery capacity.

## What the market is selling

### 1. Platform and speed

Cobalt sells annual credits equal to eight testing hours, start times tied to plan level, platform collaboration, integrations, and retesting. BreachLock sells a unified platform where findings appear during testing, a 24–48 hour start, and automated and manual retesting. The buyer is purchasing operational throughput as much as a test.

Sthuthi should not promise rapid starts until staffing makes that true. The honest counter-position is a clear ten-business-day test after scope and access are ready, with exact entry conditions. A buyer with one urgent application may prefer certainty over a new annual platform contract.

### 2. Human review of AI output

Every serious competitor limits automation claims with expert validation. Cobalt says pentesters direct autonomous testing. NetSPI sells validation of AI-tool findings. BreachLock states that certified pentesters verify exploitable findings. This is now expected, not a unique message.

Sthuthi should specify where human judgment occurs:

- scope and authorization review;
- scenario and expected-rule approval;
- review of model and tool traces;
- material-impact decision;
- severity decision;
- client-visible redaction;
- fix verification.

That is stronger than saying “human in the loop” without naming the decisions.

### 3. AI scope is moving beyond jailbreaks

The leading AI pages cover the application, model, retrieval system, data, tools, cloud, pipelines, and permissions. HackerOne separates a small AI feature from an AI-native or agentic system. Bishop Fox and Trail of Bits describe infrastructure, supply chain, data provenance, CI/CD, and agent workflows. CSIS explains the buying question in plain terms: can the system reveal data, follow hidden instructions, or take an unapproved tool action?

Sthuthi's current catalog is aligned with this shift. The phrase `Model output alone does not establish a finding` is a useful difference. Make it central. A successful jailbreak matters only when it breaks an agreed rule or creates material impact.

### 4. Proof is part of the product

Competitors use several forms of proof:

- platform screenshots and workflow detail;
- customer names and stories;
- practitioner certifications;
- testing counts and years in business;
- public research and open-source tools;
- named methodologies and standards;
- sample outputs and report descriptions;
- analyst recognition and third-party reviews.

Sthuthi currently has product evidence but little organizational proof. Do not replace missing proof with broad claims. Publish the illustrative report, method, staff facts, insurance, and real client outcomes in that order as they become available.

## Offer comparison

### Penetration testing

Current Sthuthi offer: from $12,000 for one application or API, roughly ten business days after readiness, report, walkthrough, and one retest within 30 days.

Market observations:

- Large PTaaS firms usually require a quote or annual plan.
- Current small-provider search results show fixed scopes from roughly $3,000 to $5,000 for narrow SOC 2-oriented tests, while broader specialist services publish higher ranges.
- Cobalt currently promotes an autonomous web test at $3,500, but its standard human/PTaaS program uses annual credits.
- Retesting is commonly included, sometimes without limits during a contract.

Decision: keep the $12,000 starting price only if the method, tester qualifications, depth, and deliverables support a specialist manual test. The site must explain why this is not a low-cost scan. If the first delivery team cannot demonstrate that, add a smaller readiness or validation offer rather than discount the main test without changing scope.

Recommended package structure:

| Package | Scope | Price treatment | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Web application pentest | One authenticated app, agreed roles, backing endpoints used by the app | From $12,000 | Main application offer |
| API pentest | One API, endpoint inventory, role matrix, agreed credentials | From $12,000 | Separate scope, not a footnote |
| SOC 2 web + API scope | One web app and its first-party API, exact auditor requirement | Scoped quote | Prevent scope ambiguity |
| Retest | Agreed findings within 30 days | Included once | Removes purchase uncertainty |
| Additional application, API, role set, or environment | Written change order | Priced in quote | Stops hidden scope expansion |

### AI security assessment

Current Sthuthi offer: $9,500 for one application, two critical workflows, up to five connected tools, report, walkthrough, and one retest.

This is the best opening in the catalog. Most established competitors require a sales call and custom quote. Sthuthi can make the offer easier to understand without claiming their experience or breadth.

Recommended scope choices:

| Offer | Best fit | Coverage | Commercial treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI feature add-on | Chat, summary, classification, or recommendation inside an existing product | Prompt injection, disclosure, output handling, system prompt exposure, relevant app/API controls | Add-on to application pentest; price after delivery cost is modeled |
| AI security assessment | RAG, agent, copilot, or AI-native workflow | Two critical workflows, five tools, customer boundaries, indirect injection, permissions, approvals, action traces | Keep the published $9,500 launch offer |
| Expanded agent assessment | Multi-agent system, broad tool set, sensitive autonomous actions | Additional workflows, memory, identity, cloud, data pipeline, and failure recovery | Custom scope |

Do not call the $9,500 scope a full AI red team. That term implies broader objectives, longer attack paths, infrastructure and personnel coverage, and sometimes detection testing.

### Compliance readiness and questionnaire support

Current Sthuthi offer: from $15,000 covering a named-framework gap assessment, remediation list, draft policies, evidence, and questionnaire support.

Observed competitors split this market into:

- low-cost readiness snapshots around $1,000–$1,500;
- platform setup and baseline work around $7,500;
- complete readiness programs around $12,500–$15,000 and above;
- monthly managed readiness from roughly $1,800 for very small startups to $4,500 or more;
- questionnaire-only completion with a one- to five-business-day promise.

Decision: split the current broad offer into an entry assessment and an implementation program. A buyer searching for questionnaire help should not see a $15,000 framework program as the only purchase.

Recommended structure:

| Offer | Deliverable | Proposed treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Readiness snapshot | Scope, selected evidence review, blockers, prioritized plan | Create a lower-cost fixed-scope entry after delivery hours are measured |
| Full readiness program | Control mapping, policy and evidence work, remediation tracking, pre-audit handoff | Keep from $15,000 |
| Security questionnaire support | Evidence-backed draft, unsupported answers flagged, gap list | Separate fixed-scope service or add-on |
| Technical validation | Pen test or cloud assessment linked to gaps | Separate authorized engagement |

State on every relevant page that Sthuthi does not issue the SOC 2 report, ISO certificate, or audit opinion.

## Positioning gaps and recommended response

| Competitor advantage | Sthuthi should do now | Sthuthi should not claim |
|---|---|---|
| Large pentester network and rapid scheduling | Publish honest readiness conditions and available start windows only when staffed | Equivalent global capacity |
| Mature delivery platform | Show the working scope, evidence, finding, remediation, and retest workflow | Enterprise integrations that remain unconnected |
| Certifications and public team biographies | Name qualified delivery staff and list verified credentials before sale | Generic “expert team” wording |
| Customer logos and case studies | Publish permitted cases after real delivery | Parent-company training proof as security proof |
| Public research and tools | Publish the synthetic test method and useful technical write-ups | Research leadership without a record |
| Unlimited contract retests | Include one bounded retest and price additional work clearly | Unlimited retesting without staffing and terms |
| Compliance mapping | Ask for the buyer's exact framework and auditor request | Universal audit acceptance |
| Analyst recognition | Use product evidence and specific scope | Unverified awards or rankings |

## Messaging recommendation

Primary message:

`Security testing for the apps, cloud, and AI your business runs on.`

Supporting message:

`We test whether your software and AI tools can expose customer data or take actions they should not. You get reproducible findings, help with the fixes, and a retest.`

AI-specific message:

`A jailbreak is not automatically a security finding. We record the tested version, the rule the system should enforce, the observed action, and the business impact.`

Procurement message:

`The scope, authorization, access requirements, test window, deliverables, price, and material limits are written before testing begins.`

Avoid making “AI-powered testing” the main claim. Established firms already make it, and buyers expect human validation. Sthuthi's stronger point is the quality and boundary of the evidence.

## Evidence and uncertainty record

### Observed

- Cobalt publishes annual credit mechanics, platform features, retesting, tier-based starts, and a temporary autonomous-test price.
- NetSPI publishes separate AI benchmarking, custom testing, continuous testing, and AI-finding validation services.
- HackerOne documents distinct add-on and standalone LLM application-pentest methods.
- Bishop Fox covers application, cloud, model, data, infrastructure, and red-team scenarios.
- BreachLock sells platform visibility, quick starts, in-house certified testers, and retesting.
- Trail of Bits publishes technical deliverables that extend beyond a PDF, including code rules and adversarial harnesses.
- Questionnaire services stress evidence-backed answers and flagging unknowns rather than guessing.

### Inferred

- Published Sthuthi pricing can reduce purchase friction for a midmarket buyer comparing quote-only vendors.
- Evidence quality and explicit limits can distinguish the offer more credibly than automation language.
- Separate pages for web, API, SOC 2, AI, and questionnaire intents should match buyer decisions better than one broad services page.

These are strategy inferences from the observed pages, not demand or conversion facts.

### Unknown

- US monthly search volume, paid-click cost, and ranking difficulty.
- Competitor close rates, contract values, delivery margins, and customer satisfaction beyond published material.
- Whether Sthuthi's target buyers will accept the proposed prices.
- Which tester credentials, insurance, response times, and capacity Sthuthi can verify.
- Whether any auditor or enterprise procurement team has accepted a Sthuthi report.

## Reverse-engineering investigation record

The requested `reverse-engineer-anything` skill was used as the investigation discipline for provenance, observations, inferences, unknowns, and tool limits.

- `rea doctor --client codex --json` found REA CLI 6.8.0 and the installed skill aligned at version 49.
- REA's Codex MCP registration was missing or out of date in the local client configuration. Changing that configuration requires explicit approval and a client restart, so it was not changed.
- A bounded browser capture was attempted against Cobalt's public pricing page with URL, accessibility, and DOM capture requested.
- The REA browser provider returned a non-retryable `execution_failure` and produced no evidence artifact. Cleanup reported incomplete status but listed no retained resource identifiers.
- The unchanged capture was not repeated and another REA target was not opened. Current public pages were then researched through the available read-only web tool.

No claim in this report relies on the failed REA capture.
