If you search "AI penetration testing companies" in 2027, you'll get a dozen lists that all put the list's own author at #1. That's not useless — most of the names recur for a reason — but it means you can't shortlist from search results alone. You need to know who's independent, who got acquired into a platform in the last 18 months, and who's running the same automated scanner priced like a boutique engagement.

This guide separates the two things that actually matter when you're buying: **who tests AI/LLM systems as a human-led service**, and **who got bought by whom**, because a vendor's parent company changes what you're actually buying (platform bundle vs. standalone engagement) and often its price.

## How to actually pick one

1. **Decide if you need a report or a certification.** A SOC 2 auditor wants a signed pentest report with findings and remediation evidence — see our breakdown of what a [SOC 2 penetration test](/blog/soc2-penetration-test/) needs to show. An ISO 42001 audit wants something closer to a governance review. Schellman is built for the second; most of the rest for the first.
2. **Check if agents and MCP are in scope by name.** "AI penetration testing" increasingly means testing an *agent* that calls tools, not just a chatbot. If the vendor's page doesn't mention MCP servers or agentic workflows explicitly, ask — don't assume. Our [AI penetration testing explainer](/blog/what-is-ai-penetration-testing/) covers what should be in scope.
3. **Ask who actually signs the report.** CREST accreditation, named testers, and a defined methodology matter more for audit purposes than a vendor's marketing claims about scale.
4. **Get the price anchored to a scope, not a tier name.** Our [AI security assessment cost guide](/blog/ai-security-assessment-cost/) has the real driver breakdown — read it before a sales call so a "custom quote" doesn't surprise you.

## FAQ

**What's the difference between "AI penetration testing" and regular penetration testing?**
Regular pentesting targets infrastructure, networks, and web apps. AI penetration testing specifically targets model behavior — prompt injection, jailbreaks, data leakage through outputs, agent tool misuse — risks mapped by the [OWASP LLM Top 10](/blog/owasp-llm-top-10/) that a standard web app pentest doesn't test for.

**Do I need an AI pentest if I already run a regular pentest?**
Yes, if your product has an LLM, chatbot, or agent in it. A standard pentest checks your infrastructure is locked down; it won't catch a prompt-injection path that lets one customer see another customer's data through the chat interface.

**How much does an AI penetration testing engagement cost?**
Entry-level automated scans start around $3,000; full human-led engagements with agent and RAG testing typically run $8,000–$30,000+ depending on scope. See our [cost breakdown](/blog/ai-security-assessment-cost/) for the drivers.

**Is a platform (Prisma AIRS, Lakera, Zscaler AI Protect) the same as hiring a pentest firm?**
No. Platforms run continuous automated red-teaming as a subscription, usually bundled into a larger enterprise security contract. A pentest firm delivers a scoped, time-boxed engagement with a human-signed report — the kind an auditor or customer security questionnaire usually wants.

**Can open-source tools like garak or PyRIT replace a paid pentest?**
They're excellent for internal testing between audits, but they don't produce the independently-signed report that SOC 2 auditors, enterprise customers, or cyber insurance underwriters typically require.

**What should I ask a vendor before signing?**
Ask: is agent/MCP testing in scope by default or an add-on? Who signs the report and what's their accreditation? What's excluded? Is the quote based on app complexity or a flat tier? Our [cost guide](/blog/ai-security-assessment-cost/) has the full list.

**Are vendor "ranked #1" claims on these lists trustworthy?**
Treat every self-published ranking — including implicit ones in this article — as marketing. Verify claims like client counts, accreditation, and pricing directly with the vendor or a third party like G2 before deciding.

**Does getting acquired (like Protect AI or Lakera) make a vendor worse?**
Not necessarily — it usually means more engineering resources and tighter integration with a larger security stack, but it also usually means the product is now sold as part of a platform bundle rather than standalone, which changes contracting and price.

**What's the single biggest mistake buyers make shortlisting AI pentest vendors?**
Picking based on a listicle ranking instead of checking whether agent/MCP testing, reporting format, and compliance mapping actually match what their own auditor or customer security team needs.
